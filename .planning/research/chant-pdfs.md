## 1. Table of Sarum Tones (St Dunstan pp. 499-508)

Schema is **Tone.Mediation.Ending** expressed as `Roman.Letter.Arabic`, e.g. `I A 1`, `III B 1`, `IV S 8`. Where a mode only has one mediation, the letter is elided (e.g. `II 1`, `V 1`, `VII 1`, `VIII 1`). The "anatomy" panel on p. 499 labels the five parts of a psalm tone: **Intonation → Reciting Note → Mediant Cadence → Reciting Note → Final Cadence**.

Special labels seen beside specific endings: `Gloucester` (I A 11, I B 11), `York` (IV 11, IV 14, VII 8), `Gisburn` (IV 12), `Gisburn, Worcester, York` (IV 13), `Peterborough` (VII 9). These are named after English medieval use. Plus: `Abrupt Mediation` (I B, III A, VII), `B, Intonation for Benedictus and Magnificat` (I), `Tone III B for Benedictus and Magnificat (only)`, `Intonation for Benedictus and Magnificat` under IV S and VI and VII (distinct form for Gospel canticles). Note: "Tone III A 7 is never used without an Antiphon" (p. 502).

### Tonus Peregrinus (labeled "IRREGULAR TONE", p. 507)

Printed on a dedicated page. St Dunstan treats it **separately** from the 8 regular tones. Structure shown:

- **P B** (Mediation B) — 1 row

Peregrinus is distinctive because it uses **two different reciting notes** (one for each half-verse) — visible in the notation as a clef/pitch shift mid-verse. The Plainsong Psalter (Litton) confirms this by providing a Gloria Patri for Tonus Peregrinus alongside Tones 1-8 (PS p. xvii).

- **Irregular Tone C** — 1 row + flex

### "Eight Tones" vs. "fuller tonus" — smoke-test subset

The PDF does not explicitly flag a canonical minimal subset. However, the natural "smoke-test 8" (one ending per mode, plus Peregrinus) that matches CHANT_DESIGN.md §10 would reasonably be:
`I A 1, II 1, III A 1, IV 1, V 1, VI A (or VI B), VII 1, VIII 1` + `P A` for Peregrinus.

Additional observations relevant to the data model:
- **Mediation letters in use:** `A`, `B`, `C` (only Tone VI has C), `S` (Solemn, distinct from A/B/C — used on feast days), plus "Abrupt" variants for Tones I-B, III-A, VII.
- **Ending labels:** integer, 1-14 (Tone IV goes highest). Some carry proper-name labels (Gloucester, York, Gisburn, Worcester, Peterborough). Single-mediation modes (II, V, VII, VIII) format as `II 1` etc., with no letter.
- **Gospel-canticle intonation:** every mode provides a distinct "Intonation for Benedictus and Magnificat." This is the `useFor: ['benedictus','magnificat']` case already in `PsalmTone`. Tone III goes further and has an entirely separate mediation form (`III B for Benedictus and Magnificat (only)`).
- **Flex:** every mode/Peregrinus/Irregular has a single flex formula — supports CHANT_DESIGN decision 3 (flex derived, not a stored field).

## 2. Pointing conventions

### St Dunstan (from `concerning the psalter.pdf` = Notes on the Plainchant, pp. xv-xx, and `psalm example.pdf` p. 14)

The St Dunstan pointing system is explicit and typographical. Key rubrics quoted verbatim (p. xv):

- **Dot (·)** — placed before the first syllable of each Mediation and Ending. *"The singer need remember only that with the syllable following the Dot he leaves the reciting note and begins a melodic cadence."* This is the **mediant/final cadence marker**. In the psalm example, the dots appear inline as "the · children of men" marking where cadence starts.
- **Asterisk (\*)** — marks the Caesura, the central pause of the psalm verse (between half-verses). Corresponds to the full bar in the tone.
- **Flex (†)** — marks a short pause for breath within the first half-verse on long verses. Used only when the first half is too long for one breath.
- **Accent marks above syllables:**
  - **[ ⁻ ]** (bar over syllable) — "used when the syllable is strong (accented) and therefore *uninflected*" before a Flex
  - **[ ˇ ]** (breve/caron) — "used to indicate weak (unaccented) syllables which *are* inflected"
  - Implicitly bolded text in the psalm example at "begin·ning" for Gloria Patri mediation — may be the accented syllable on the cadence note (needs confirmation from additional psalm pages)

Pointing is at **syllable level, not word level** — dots appear mid-word (e.g. "a·bominable", "begin·ning"). Preparatory unaccented syllables between dot and cadence are implicit (sung on "parenthetic hollow/white notes" — p. xvii §6: *"Where extra unaccented syllables occur, the parenthetic notes (the hollow 'white' notes) will be used, as needed"*).

**Trochaic vs dactylic** is not named as such in St Dunstan. Instead: p. xvii §6: *"In the Mediations of Tones II, IV, V, VI C, and VIII, the notes following the notes marked with an accent are omitted when the first half of the Psalm Verse ends with an accented syllable. Only the number of notes required by the number of syllables in the words is sung."* This is the rule that yields dactylic vs trochaic endings implicitly — it's syllable-counted, not flagged as a type.

**Gloria Patri** (p. xvi): *"The two verses of Gloria Patri are sung precisely like the others; without preliminary retard in the preceding Verse or pause after it; and without the use of the Intonation."*

**Hyphen in "-ed" endings** (p. xix §5 Pronunciation): *"The last word in the Mediant or Ending may be a past participle, ending with the syllable '-ed'… The hyphen printed is rather longer than is usual in printing to reduce the likelihood of its being mistaken for the Dot in dim light."* (So: `look-ed`, `enlighten-ed`, `strengthen-ed`, `ad-ver-sar-ies` in the Collect tones; confirmed visible in the psalm example and collect examples — St Dunstan hyphens every historical -ed even when a modern reader would elide it.)

**Psalm 14 example** (p. 14): Verse 1 reads *"The fool hath · said in his heart, \* · There is no God."* — the dot marks where the cadence starts in each half-verse. The tone assignment is printed above the staff: `Psalm 14 (13) Dixit insipiens in corde suo Tone VII 4`. So St Dunstan's psalter **prints the tone assignment at the head of each psalm**.

### The Plainsong Psalter (Litton, 1988, "Performance Notes", pp. xi-xii)

Litton uses a different, more complex typographical system:

- **Italic type** — "indicates the intonation" (the first two syllables/words sung to the intonation of the first verse). E.g. in Ps 1:1 *"Happy are they…"* `Happy are` is italic.
- **Accent mark (´) over syllables** — "Accent marks in the pointed text (´) correspond to accent marks under the notes in the psalm tone (ř)." — marks accented syllables at the mediant and final cadences. Multiple accents per half-verse show both the accented and preparatory syllables.
- **Diagonal bar ( / )** before a syllable — "Preparatory syllables follow the diagonal bar in the text." These are unaccented syllables sung to the parenthetic notes.
- **Asterisk (\*)** — marks the mediant (mid-verse caesura), same role as in St Dunstan.
- **Parenthetic notes (·) under the tone** — unaccented notes used "only when needed"; when the half-verse ends on an accented syllable, the final note is dropped (an "abrupt mediation").
- **Dots over individual syllables** (e.g. "set upón your thröne" for Ps Tone Id) — marks syllables that require 2+ notes slurred together.
- **Dash ( — )** in the pointed text — used when the Intonation or Reciting Note is omitted due to a short half-verse.

So the two systems diverge markedly in notation but encode equivalent information:

| Concept | St Dunstan | Plainsong Psalter (Litton) |
|---|---|---|
| Start of mediant/final cadence | Dot (·) before syllable | Diagonal bar (/) + accent marks |
| Accented syllable | Implicit (natural word accent) | Explicit acute (´) over syllable |
| Caesura | Asterisk (\*) | Asterisk (\*) |
| Flex | Dagger (†) | Not observed in pages read (may differ) |
| Intonation | Implicit (verse 1 only) | Italic type on verse 1 |
| Extra preparatory syllables | Parenthetic hollow notes in tone | (/) bar + parenthetic notes in tone |
| Accented vs unaccented over flex | ⁻ vs ˇ marks | N/A in pages read |

Both systems support the algorithmic pointer in CHANT_DESIGN.md §12, but map to different output renderers:

- For a `PsalmPointing.mediantAccent` / `finalAccent` field (word index from end), **St Dunstan's dot-before-cadence-syllable** is *syllable-indexed*, while **Litton's acute accent** is *syllable-indexed* but also identifies the exact accented syllable within the cadence word. CHANT_DESIGN's word-from-end index approximates both if combined with a vowel heuristic (decision 6).
- St Dunstan does NOT have an explicit "dactylic" flag — the tone's written-out abrupt form handles it. Litton uses the parenthetic-note convention with no textual flag either.

**Therefore the `ending: 'dactylic'` field in `PsalmPointing`** is a Venite-specific abstraction; neither published psalter uses that label. It will need to be derived algorithmically from the accent position on the last word and confirmed by ear/checkpoint.

## 3. Tone-to-psalm assignment rules

### St Dunstan

**Tones are assigned per-psalm and printed at the head of each psalm** (psalm example: Psalm 14 = Tone VII 4; Psalm 15 = Tone III A 5). This is confirmed by `concerning the daily office .pdf` p. 233 where it says *"Every time the Antiphon is sung according to a Psalm tone, the intonation is sung…"* — implying each psalm has a pre-assigned tone.

Neither `concerning the psalter.pdf` nor `concerning the daily office .pdf` explicitly publishes a **rule** for how to select the tone (e.g. "tone follows the antiphon mode"). The St Dunstan volume simply publishes its own *table of assignments* inline at the head of each psalm. The rule behind the table is not printed.

The common Anglican custom (not stated in these PDFs but standard in the tradition, per CHANT_DESIGN §9) is: **tone follows the mode of the antiphon**, when an antiphon is sung. When no antiphon is sung, the tone is editor's choice. St Dunstan appears to apply this custom in its fixed tone-per-psalm assignments.

**Canticle Antiphons** (`concerning the daily office .pdf` p. 237): *"The appropriate Antiphons on Magnificat and Benedictus, set to their proper plainsong tunes, may be found in The Monastic Diurnal Noted."* — St Dunstan **does not publish antiphon mode tables**; it references the Monastic Diurnal Noted.

### The Plainsong Psalter (Litton)

Litton **does publish a full psalm→tone assignment table** by assigning one tone per psalm printed at the head of each psalm page (Ps 1 → Tone V.1, Ps 2 → Tone VIII.1, Ps 3 → Tone II.1, Ps 4 → Tone IV.1, Ps 5 → Tone VI, Ps 6 → Tone II.1, etc.). Notation is `Tone [Mode Roman].[Ending Arabic]` with **no explicit mediation letter** — Litton's system collapses to one mediation per mode.

Preface (p. v): Litton followed *"Dr. Crocker's choice of psalm tones as well as his method of pointing the psalm tones. Where this has not been possible, many plainsong psalters have been consulted, from Helmore's A Manual of Plainsong, 1850 to Willan's The Canadian Psalter, 1963."* So Litton's assignments are an editorial amalgam building on Crocker's *Gradual Psalms* tradition, not a mechanical rule.

Antiphons in Litton are provided per-psalm at the top of each page ("In Easter Season", "On other Sundays and Weekdays", sometimes a seasonal variant like "On the Twelve Days of Christmas" for Ps 2, "In Holy Week" for Ps 6). These are melodic antiphons from the ancient repertory (preface p. xiv: *"dating from the period circa A.D. 400-700"*).

**Structure of Litton's assignment table**: implicit — each psalm page has `Psalm N [Latin incipit] Tone X.Y` as a header. No seasonal variation of tone assignments; only the antiphon varies seasonally. This is consistent with CHANT_DESIGN.md's `PsalmToneAssignment` record (which has optional `season`, `day`, `evening` fields but does not require them).

### Differences between Mattins / Evensong / Compline

`concerning the daily office .pdf` describes no mode/tone-selection difference between Mattins and Evensong — both use the appointed Psalm of the Day from the monthly BCP cycle and chant it to its assigned tone.

**Compline** (`an order for compline.pdf` p. 288) lists fixed psalms: **Ps 4, 31:I, 91, 134**. The compline PDF does not print tone assignments for these psalms (they would be found elsewhere in the St Dunstan volume at the normal psalter pages). Notably, **Compline's Nunc Dimittis is explicitly assigned Tone III A 4** (p. 290: *"Then shall the Canticle be chanted to Tone III A 4 (p. 478), with its Antiphon before and after"*). So even canticles get an explicit tone, and in some cases pre-specified.

### Per-verse vs per-psalm

Both St Dunstan and the Plainsong Psalter apply **one tone per psalm**, not changing mid-psalm. However, **long psalms split into sections** (Ps 119 Aleph…Tau; Ps 18:1 vs 18:2) in BCP 1979 would each get their own tone assignment — CHANT_DESIGN's `PsalmToneAssignment.psalm: "119-aleph"` and `partNumber` fields map to this.

## 4. Gloria Patri

From `concerning the psalter.pdf` p. xvi: *"The two verses of Gloria Patri are sung precisely like the others; without preliminary retard in the preceding Verse or pause after it; and without the use of the Intonation."*

Therefore:
- **Same tone as the psalm** — confirmed (matches CHANT_DESIGN decision 4, no special GP field needed).
- **No intonation** on the Gloria Patri (unlike verse 1 of the psalm).
- **No retard or pause** before or during it.

The Plainsong Psalter (pp. xv-xvii) prints a **separate Gloria Patri line** for each of the 8 tones + Peregrinus — not because the GP uses a different tone, but so singers can see the GP pointing worked out for each tone's cadence. Each tone has one ending shown; "Different endings can be pointed based on the examples given in the chart" (p. xv).

**Special rules:**

- **Passiontide** (`concerning the daily office .pdf` p. 233): *"During Passiontide, or from Passion Sunday to Wednesday in Holy Week (inclusive), the Gloria Patri should not be recited here [at the Venite] (except when the day is a festival)."* This omission rule applies specifically to the Venite (Invitatory Psalm at Matins). The PDFs do not specify an omission rule for the Gloria Patri on the Psalms of the Day during Passiontide (customarily it is omitted then too, but that rule is not printed here).
- **Office of the Dead / All Souls' Day / Requiem** (p. 233): *"In Matins of the Dead and on All Souls' Day, the Venite is said, and the verses 'Rest eternal grant unto them, O Lord / And may light perpetual shine upon them' replace the Gloria Patri."* So for requiems, **"Rest eternal" substitutes for the Gloria Patri** — same tone as the psalm, different text. This is content, not tone-data.
- **No separate Gloria Patri tone** in St Dunstan.

Lent is not mentioned as special for the Gloria Patri. "Alleluia" is suppressed during Lent but the PDFs do not single out the Gloria Patri for different treatment.

## 5. Collect tones (`chant of the collects .pdf`, pp. 276-285)

**Two distinct methods** are defined:

### A. The Ancient Method (§2, p. 276)

*"Unlike the later Collect Tones, makes no distinction between Festal … and Ferial … This Tone traditionally is used for the Collects at Mass, as well as the Collects at the Daily Office. It is used on any day in any season."*

Structure: **Reciting Note (Recitation) → Flex → Full Stop (Punctum) → Final Cadence (Conclusion)**.

Printed sub-components (p. 276):
1. **The Collect Flex** — single neume drop
2. **The Collect Full Stop** (also called "Punctum" or "Cadence") — descending figure on last accented syllable of a sentence/clause
3. **The Collect Final Cadence** (also called "Conclusion") — only at the doxology before "Amen"

Rubrics: *"The first syllable of the Collect is always sung one whole step lower than the Reciting Note."* (p. 276). *"The Flex is made at the first or second sub-division in the Collect."* *"The Full Stop is made at the conclusion of the sentence or independent clause, such as at the period or semi-colon."* *"The Final Cadence is used at the conclusion of the Doxology, immediately before the 'Amen.'"* *"the Full Stop is never used without the Flex preceding it."* (p. 277).

The ancient tone also includes **tones for the Salutation and Response**:
- V. The Lord be with you. R. And with thy spirit. V. Let us pray. — one formula
- (For laymen/non-ordained) V. O Lord, hear our prayer. R. And let our cry come unto thee. V. Let us pray. — alternate formula (p. 278)

### B. The Later Method (§2, p. 281)

*"Perhaps late medieval but probably modern (15th to 16th centuries), does make a distinction between the Festal tone (i.e. on Sundays and Holy Days) and the Ferial tone."*

**Ferial Tone** (p. 281): *"quite simple - the Collect is sung monotone throughout, with no inflections or cadences … suitable to non-feasts and fast days."* — Pure recto tono, no neumes.

**Festal Tone** (pp. 281-282): uses two simple inflections — **Metrum (Cadence)** and **Flex**. Structure follows the theological parts of a Collect: Address, Petition, Oblation, Doxology.

- **Metrum** — applied to the closing syllables of the **Address** and to the closing syllables of the **Doxology** (i.e. start and end of the Collect)
- **Flex** — applied to the **Petition** and the **Oblation** (one or two notes one step below the Reciting Note)

Rubric (p. 282): *"The Metrum usually occurs at the end of the first part of the Collect, and always at the very end of the Collect before the 'Amen.' … The Metrum is applied to the first phrase of the Collect, the Flex to the phrases in the middle of the Collect, and the Metrum again to the last phrase."*

### Fully-worked examples printed in St Dunstan

Pointed with GABC-equivalent neumes (pp. 278-280 for Ancient method; 283-285 for Later method):

1. **Collect for Peace at Matins** — *Deus Auctor pacis* — "O GOD, who art the author of peace…"
2. **Collect for Grace at Matins** — *Domine sancte* — "O LORD, our heavenly Father, Almighty and everlasting God…"
3. **Collect for Peace at Evensong** — *Deus, a quo sancta* — "O GOD, from whom all holy desires…"
4. **Collect for Aid at Evensong (for Aid against Perils)** — *Illumina, quaesumus* — "LIGHTEN our darkness…"

Each collect is worked out in BOTH the Ancient and Later methods (with the headings "FOR AID AT EVENSONG" etc.) giving a total of **8 printed collect chant examples** (4 collects × 2 methods).

CHANT_DESIGN's collect inventory (decision 15: ferial + festal) can be mapped thus:
- **Ferial tone** = pure recto tono (no GABC needed — rendered as CSS flat-line text, or a single-pitch GABC)
- **Festal tone** = the "Later Method" with Metrum + Flex
- If the "Ancient Method" is to be offered as a third option, that adds Flex + Full Stop + Final Cadence.

**Total discrete collect-tone chant elements to transcribe to GABC:** Ancient (flex, full stop, final cadence, salutation tone, "O Lord hear our prayer" tone) = 5 fragments. Later festal (metrum, flex, address-intonation stepdown) = 3 fragments. Plus the 4 fully-worked fixed-collect examples (8 if both methods). Realistically, **3-5 fragment templates** cover all collect chanting via compositional substitution.

## 6. Lesson tones (`chanting the lessons.pdf`, pp. 270-275)

Only **one** Daily-Office Lesson Tone is defined in St Dunstan. §2 p. 270: *"The most ancient Lesson Tone (originally devised for the Scriptural, Patristic and Hagiographical Lessons at Matins in the Roman Divine Office) has a pattern of notes identical with some of the Tones for the Epistle and Gospel at Mass. However, the intervals between pitches in the Lesson Tone are not the same as those in the Eucharistic formulae."*

No OT/Epistle/Gospel distinction at the Office — it is the same tone for both Lessons, even though the Daily Office has a First (OT) and Second (NT/Epistle) Lesson.

### Structure (p. 270)

Three melodic cadences:
1. **Final Cadence (Full Stop)** — descending figure at end of a sentence
2. **Flex** — mid-sentence
3. **Metrum** — end of a clause (semicolon/colon separating clauses)

### Rules for applying cadences (pp. 271-273)

- **One-sentence / no natural pause** → only (3) Final Cadence used. *"Even so, come Lord Jesus."* / *"Jesus wept."* / *"They sat down."*
- **One pause** → (2) Metrum midway + (3) Full Stop at end. *"But his wife look-ed back from behind him, (Metrum) and she became a pillar of salt. (Full Stop)"*
- **Two pauses** → (1) Flex, (2) Metrum, (3) Full Stop. *"Grudge not one against another, brethren, (Flex) lest ye be condemn-ed: (Metrum) behold, the judge standeth before the door. (Full Stop)"*
- **Multiple pauses** → Flex and Metrum alternate as many times as needed, ending with Full Stop.
- *"The Metrum may be used without the Flex. But the Flex is not used if the Metrum is not used in the sentence."* (p. 273)

### Question Tone (pp. 273-274)

A separate formula for **questions within the text**. *"In the Question Tone, as in all plainsong formulae, if the words are too few to employ the entire tune, the notes are used in this manner: A one-syllable sentence uses only the last note… a two-syllable sentence employs the last two notes,"* etc. Examples: *"What house will ye build me?"* *"What think ye of Christ?"* *"What hast thou done?"* (one-word questions use only the final pitch).

Exception: *"If the last sentence or clause of a Lesson is a Question, the Question Tone is not used for the final sentence or clause."* (p. 274 — the lesson closes with the normal Final Cadence regardless).

### Conclusion

*"the conclusion, 'Here endeth the First [or, Second] Lesson' is always spoken without note"* (p. 275). So the conclusion is **not** chanted — it is a spoken rubric.

### Daily Office vs Mass

Used at **both** the Office and Mass per the PDF, BUT the Mass Epistle and Gospel tones have different intervals (p. 270 explicit). Only one tone to transcribe for the Daily Office.

**Total discrete lesson-tone fragments:** Flex, Metrum, Full Stop (Final Cadence), Question Tone = **4 fragments**. All single-syllable/word elasticated formulae — simple to encode.

## 10. Open questions & decisions needed

1. **Tone naming/ID format cross-mapping** — CHANT_DESIGN uses `tone-1-a-4` (Mode.Mediation.Ending), which fits St Dunstan's `I A 4` perfectly. But Litton's Plainsong Psalter uses `Tone V.1` (no mediation letter), and for St Dunstan single-mediation modes the letter is elided (`II 1`, `V 1`). Decision needed: **When a mode has only one mediation, is the slug `tone-2-1` or `tone-2-default-1`?** Recommendation: match the design doc's `mediant: "default"` string for single-mediation tones, so slug is `tone-2-default-1`.

2. **Irregular tones A, B, C** — these are genuinely-used tones in the English tradition but not labeled with a mode number. CHANT_DESIGN schema assumes `mode: number | string`. Recommendation: `mode: 'irregular-a'` or similar string.

3. **Peregrinus has two reciting notes** — unlike other tones, Tonus Peregrinus uses a different pitch for each half-verse. The `PsalmTone.recitingNote: string` (singular) in CHANT_DESIGN cannot express this. Recommendation: promote to `recitingNote: string | [string, string]` OR add `recitingNote2?: string`.

4. **Solemn vs ordinary forms** — each mode has a Solemn form (`I S`, `II S`, etc.) used on feast days for Gospel canticles. These differ structurally, not just by ornamentation. The existing `useFor: string[]` field with `['benedictus','magnificat']` partially covers this, but the **festal/ferial distinction** at the office level (not just canticle level) is separate. Recommendation: add `solemn?: boolean` flag OR an `occasion: 'ordinary' | 'solemn'` field. Further, Tone III has a distinct mediation B for Gospel canticles — so the `mediant` field must be able to carry that.

5. **Abrupt Mediations** (`I B Abrupt`, `III A Abrupt`, `VII Abrupt`) — these are specialized alternatives when the first half-verse ends in a stressed monosyllable. Not a separate ending — a separate *mediation form*. Recommendation: encode as `mediantCadenceGabc` variants keyed by the pointing condition, OR as a separate `PsalmTone` entry with an `abrupt: true` flag and the same `finalCadence` attached.

6. **Named-tradition endings** (Gloucester, York, Gisburn, Worcester, Peterborough) — these are regional English Sarum variants of specific ending numbers. CHANT_DESIGN's `label?: string` field handles this. Decision: should the `finalCadence` field carry the number only (`"11"`) and `label` carry `"Gloucester"`, or should `finalCadence` be `"Gloucester"`? Recommendation: number in `finalCadence`, tradition name in `label`.

7. **Gloria Patri** — confirmed no special tone needed (decision 4). However, the **Litton Gloria Patri chart** (PS pp. xv-xvii) shows worked-out Gloria Patri settings for each tone. These could be useful as reference renderings but are not required data — they're re-derivable from the parent tone formula.

8. **Ferial collect tone = recto tono** — this needs no neume data, only a flag. CHANT_DESIGN's `gabc?: string` field should accept an empty-GABC representation OR the collect tone should be resolved at render time from a mode setting.

9. **Lesson Question Tone elasticity** — the Question Tone uses "the last N notes" where N = min(syllables, total-notes). This is a runtime substring, not a per-lesson stored GABC. Render strategy: the lesson tone engine should compute the tail-slice at render time from the question's syllable count.

10. **Pointing: St Dunstan dots are syllable-internal** (e.g. "a·bominable", "begin·ning"). CHANT_DESIGN's `PsalmPointing.mediantAccent: number` is word-indexed. The `point-psalm.ts` algorithm will need a vowel heuristic for sub-word accent position (decision 6 handles this), but confirm: **is the dot marking the start of the cadence or the accented syllable of the last-accented word?** From the rubrics: the dot marks the *first syllable on which the cadence notes begin*, which may be a preparatory unaccented syllable. This is not the same as the "accented word-from-end" model in CHANT_DESIGN. The algorithmic pointer must derive the dot position from the accent position + number of preparatory syllables.

11. **Pointing: flex accent markers** — St Dunstan uses two marks before the flex (⁻ for strong/uninflected, ˇ for weak/inflected). CHANT_DESIGN's `flexAccent: number` only records position, not inflection type. Recommendation: either add `flexInflected?: boolean` OR accept that rendering can derive it by looking up the tone's flex interval rule (p. xvi Tones I/IV/VI/VII/Peregrinus/Irregular C → whole step below; II/III/V/VIII/Irregular A/B → minor third below — with the inflection applied only on weak syllables).

12. **Dactylic vs trochaic** — neither St Dunstan nor Litton name these. Both systems handle them implicitly: St Dunstan by having the singer use "only the number of notes required by the number of syllables" (p. xvii §6); Litton by marking accents and diagonal bars on preparatory syllables. CHANT_DESIGN's explicit `ending: 'dactylic'` flag (decision 2) is an *abstraction over* both systems — it must be derived algorithmically, not pulled from either PDF.

13. **Marian antiphon language conflict** — CHANT_DESIGN decision 14 says "Latin only from GregoBase CC0; English deferred." St Dunstan publishes **English only**. Decision needed: do we take St Dunstan's English (manual transcription, Douglas's adaptations) in phase 3, or do we defer it and take only the Latin GregoBase in phase 3? The English settings are different melodies adapted to the English text (not simple text substitution), so they are genuinely distinct content.

14. **Te Deum** — CHANT_DESIGN mentions it as a canticle but doesn't specify through-composed (Ambrosian) vs psalm-tone. St Dunstan offers both. Recommendation: ship the Ambrosian through-composed GABC (available from GregoBase) as the default; psalm-tone as a fallback via the canticle-psalm-tone override mechanism (decision 5).

15. **OT Canticles at Matins** — two-per-day (festal + ferial) for every weekday from the old Lauds cycle, plus Benedicite and Benedictus es. That's 16+ canticles, not yet in scope of CHANT_DESIGN but they are assumed to use simple psalm-tone settings.
